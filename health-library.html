Visit Prep

Before your first visit

Bring identification if available, a current medication list, prior records that may help, questions you want answered, and a trusted family member or caregiver if you need support.

  • Write symptoms in your own words.
  • List when the problem started and what makes it better or worse.
  • Bring bottles or photos of medications and supplements.
  • Do not use website forms for urgent symptoms.

Heart Health

Blood pressure basics

Blood pressure is one piece of your health story. One reading can be affected by stress, pain, caffeine, movement, and the cuff size, so tracking and context matter.

  • Ask what your numbers mean for your age, history, and risk factors.
  • Bring home readings if you have them.
  • Ask when you should recheck or follow up.

Diabetes Education

Prediabetes and diabetes basics

Learning early can help patients ask better questions about blood sugar, food choices, activity, medications, and follow-up testing.

  • Understand the difference between screening and diagnosis.
  • Track questions about thirst, urination, fatigue, and family history.
  • Ask what lifestyle changes are realistic for you.

Prevention

Preventive care guide

Preventive care means looking for problems earlier, organizing risk factors, and helping patients understand what follow-up may be needed.

  • Blood pressure checks.
  • Medication review.
  • Health education and referrals.
  • Discussion of screening needs based on age and history.

Medication Safety

Build a safer medication list

A clear medication list helps reduce confusion. Include prescription medications, over-the-counter drugs, vitamins, herbal supplements, dose, timing, and why you take each one.
